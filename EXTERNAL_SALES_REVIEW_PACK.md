## Proposed learning outcomes (observable in fictional practice)
1. Identify the seller and purpose truthfully; separate permitted contact from assumed consent.
2. Conduct nonleading discovery and confirm the buyer's actual requested outcome.
3. Qualify relevant need, purchasing process, time horizon, compliance and delivery capacity.
4. Compute a conservative, base, and optimistic value case while separating assumptions, revenue, contribution and cash collections.
5. Draft a bounded, written proposal including deliverables, exclusions, account ownership, acceptance tests and a voluntary next action.
6. Respond to objections respectfully without coercion, fabricated urgency or unsupported claims.
7. Stop contact when asked and maintain compliant recordkeeping; recognize regulated selling boundaries.
8. Self-audit a simulated conversation and revise after structured feedback.

## Assessment mechanism — limitations are explicit
- Learners complete one-question knowledge checks and their own fictional written exercises.
- Four private Sales Arena practice scenarios cover discovery, objections, closing and appointment setting. Scores are **heuristic practice indicators** and not validated psychometric measures of sales competency.
- To submit a private capstone, learners must first self-complete three prerequisite lessons, actively consent to human staff review, and supply fictional text without customer details.
- A reviewer evaluates five dimensions from 0–20. Proposed *internal provisional* threshold is total >=80/100 AND every category >=12/20. A failed dimension requires revision even when the total is high.
- No review may automatically assign work, give a professional license, promise earnings or mark the Academy accredited. All internally scored capstones remain internally labeled, not externally certified.

## Five-domain assessment rubric
Each domain is rated 0–20. Below are proposed quality anchors for external reviewer critique.

| Domain | 0–5 (insufficient) | 6–11 (developing) | 12–16 (adequate) | 17–20 (strong) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity, consent & boundaries | Fabricated identity or ignored refusal | Unclear permissions and audience | Truthful identification, basic compliance check | Explicit permitted channel, no-pressure opening, documented opt-out plan |
| Buyer discovery | Assumes problem, pitches immediately | Vague general questions | Specific problem, outcomes and authority explored | Buyer confirms baseline, constraints and decision process |
| Evidence and value | Unsupported promise or false ROI | Arithmetic without assumptions | Labeled assumptions, plausible scenario math | Three scenarios, contributions, uncertainty and proof plan |
| Proposal & ownership | No verifiable scope | Incomplete deliverables or costs | Written scope, price, exclusions and acceptance criteria | Clearly bounded tested deliverables, ownership, approvals and change control |
| Ethical objection & next step | Coercion, deception or false urgency | Weak listening or pushy close | Respectful response and voluntary next step | Correctly validates objection, cites evidence, offers reversible option and respects no |

**Safety exclusion:** Deceptive or unlawful conduct invalidates an assessment regardless of point total. Existing automated scoring is not treated as the human rubric.

## External reviewer brief
Preferred reviewer: 5+ years of verifiable B2B selling, sales leadership, sales enablement, or university-level professional sales pedagogy, preferably with adult-learning assessment design. Disclose conflicts and any compensation. A reviewer should not be asked to guarantee a favorable outcome or misrepresent an affiliation.

Reviewer work requested:
1. Review program map, original lessons, fictional case exercises, simulations and student instructions.
2. Evaluate factual accuracy, lawful contact boundaries, ethical selling and prohibited income claims.
3. Assess whether the learning outcomes are actually measured by the tools and whether the rubric is defensible.
4. Inspect anonymized sample submissions *with learner authorization*, and separately score them. Compare scores to improve inter-rater reliability.
5. Provide a dated written recommendation: approve with changes, hold pending revisions, or reject. Clearly list defects, required changes and reviewer credentials/affiliation with permission for public attribution.
6. Re-review revisions before any statement that the curriculum was **independently expert-reviewed**.

**Independent review is not accreditation.** No reviewer has been appointed and no review has been completed as of this document.

## Learner privacy, accessibility, and fairness
- Preferences are member-controlled and not used for hiring or permissions; users can erase their preferences and Academy/assessment data.
- Private capstones require explicit review consent. No actual prospect, client, banking or credential information is collected in the exercises.
- The platform should be tested using non-admin Discord accounts and learners who prefer reading, videos and practice. Accessibility, message discoverability and mobile behavior need independent testing.
- Assessment needs evidence of consistent treatment across experience levels, accessible accommodations, reasonable completion times and human appeal / second-review procedure before issuing any meaningful credential.
- Trial dashboards must separate lessons started, completed, staff graded, reviewer observations, real external work and actual commercial revenue.

## Suggested quality evidence for first pilot

- At least 10 fictional capstone submissions reviewed by two qualified people independently, subject to consent, to test rubric agreement and identify shortcomings.

- Real dated supervisor sign-offs; no auto-awarded expert titles.

## External accreditation pathway (future)
IACET accredits eligible continuing education/training **providers** through a formal process; it does not independently certify an individual course. Eligibility usually requires a legal organization operating for at least a year, with the documented continuing training program operating under application conditions for at least three months, plus policies and review evidence. See official eligibility and application process:
- https://iacet.org/ce-t-accreditation/eligibility/
- https://iacet.org/ce-t-accreditation/application-process/
- https://iacet.org/ce-t-accreditation/frequently-asked-questions/

Formal institutional accreditation (such as ACCET) has different operating-history and regulatory requirements and is not implied by university partnerships or faculty feedback.

Until a legitimate accreditation is granted, marketing must say **not accredited**, and the program may provide only accurately described internal progress or completion records, with no IACET CEUs or external certification implied.
